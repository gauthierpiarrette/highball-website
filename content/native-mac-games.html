Games that already run natively on Mac

About one in five of the "can I play X on my Mac" questions in a year of r/macgaming posts are about a game that already ships a Mac build. The answer in those threads is not Highball. It is: open the store page, buy the Mac version, play it tonight.

A native build beats translation every time. Nothing sits between the game and Metal, so you get the frame rate the developer shipped, working controllers, and updates that do not break on the next macOS release. Highball exists for the games that never got one. Checking first costs thirty seconds and saves an evening.

Checked against Steam store pages, Mac App Store listings and publisher pages on 2026-08-29. Compatibility rots. If a row here is wrong, say so and it gets fixed.

Buying a game twice is annoying. It still beats running the Windows build through a translation layer, and it is why the platform icons on the page you are buying from matter more than a general answer.

Native, but an Intel build under Rosetta

A Mac build that predates Apple Silicon still runs, translated by Rosetta 2, and that is a shorter path than Wine plus a Direct3D layer. It is on a clock: Apple has said macOS 27 is the last release with full Rosetta 2, after which a reduced subset stays behind for older unmaintained games.

  • Dota 2 supported Valve still ships and supports the Mac client. It is an x86_64 binary.
  • StarCraft II Battle.net Mac client, Intel build.
  • Civilization VI The Mac App Store version went universal in September 2024. The Steam build still lists Intel requirements, and we did not confirm its architecture.
  • Stardew Valley, Slay the Spire (the first one), Terraria, Divinity: Original Sin 2 Definitive Edition, Hades (the first one) Mac builds from the Intel era.
  • Disco Elysium Reported Apple Silicon native on the Mac App Store build and Intel on Steam. Community-reported, unverified by us.

The Valve row is where a compatibility layer genuinely helps: those Windows builds still run. Portal 2 is one of the five titles in our database verified by hand on Apple Silicon, at 200+ fps under DXVK once HDR is off. Grand Theft Auto V is the other common ask, and the answer there is split rather than native: story mode works, Online is blocked.

How to check a game yourself

  • On a Steam store page, a macOS icon means a Mac build exists. Open System Requirements, Mac tab: "Requires an Apple processor" or an M-series chip means Apple Silicon native, an Intel Core listing means Rosetta.
  • On the Mac App Store, the Compatibility section spells it out: "Mac with Apple M1 chip or later".
  • The publisher's own page is the tiebreaker when Steam and the App Store disagree.

Which should you use

If your game appears in the first two tables, install the native build and skip Highball for that title. There is no argument here: a Mac build is faster, quieter on battery, and will not break when the next macOS ships.

Do not pick Highball if you are on an Intel Mac or macOS 13 or earlier, because it needs Apple Silicon and macOS 14 or newer. Do not pick it for a game walled off by kernel anti-cheat, because nothing in user space fixes that. Do not pick it when the answer is a Mac App Store purchase you were going to enjoy more anyway.

Highball is for the middle case: a Windows-only game, no Mac build, no kernel anti-cheat. Free, GPL-3, no account, no telemetry, with a per-bottle choice of DXVK, DXMT or D3DMetal.
